# Alamut

A courtyard for sound. A handpan practice space with six Persian mountain and courtyard settings. The library lists every pattern with search and kind filters. Each row opens one piece workspace without playing audio. On phones, a compact courtyard preview leaves room for browsing; a selected piece gives the handpan and active score priority.

Practice patterns for the Gear4music DISC 10-note D Kurd handpan. The ding is D3. The tone fields are A3, B-flat3, C4, D4, E4, F4, G4, A4, and C5, at 440 Hz.

The Songs filter includes **Nothing Else Matters (Intro)** by Metallica: the whole intro, from the fingerpicked arpeggio through the lead section, the D minor, F and G add 9 turn, the returning arpeggio and the second-guitar section to the harmonic chime before the first verse, as the picking-guitar part in A minor. It is written to be played with a guitarist on capo 5 using the record's fingering, so both instruments share one key. The arpeggio is `1 3 5 8 5 3` (A3, C4, E4, A4, E4, C4), the record's exact intervals, in 6/8 at 48 dotted-quarter BPM; the record is near 46. The lead melody stays with the guitar because its notes sit above this pan; chord stabs use two notes. Open it in Play or Learn at `#/p/nothing-else-matters`. **Seven Nation Army** by The White Stripes is there too: the riff in D minor as `4 4 6 4 3 2 1` at 124 BPM, four times alone and four times with the ding under it, at `#/p/seven-nation-army`. Three more songs follow: **House of the Rising Sun** in A minor, 6/8 at 76 BPM, with the ding as the D minor bass (`#/p/house-of-the-rising-sun`); **Hallelujah** in F major, the pan's relative major, verse and chorus in 6/8 at 56 BPM, with the last Hallelujah closing upward to F because the pan has no G or F below the low A (`#/p/hallelujah`); and **Gole Sangam** in D minor, 6/8 at 60 BPM, with C on 9 standing in for the one B-flat above A that the pan lacks (`#/p/gole-sangam`). **In the End** by Linkin Park, the piano riff one semitone down in D minor at 105 BPM over D minor, B-flat, F and C (`#/p/in-the-end`). **Amazing Grace** in F major, 3/4 counted in six at 48 for the bar, is pentatonic and needs no substitution (`#/p/amazing-grace`). **Knockin' on Heaven's Door** in F major at 70 BPM keeps Dylan's four chords and the sung line on 3, 4 and 5 (`#/p/knockin-on-heavens-door`). **Soltan-e Ghalbha (Chorus)** in D minor at 96 BPM is the chorus only, with C on 9 standing in for the B-flat above A and a modal cadence in place of the C-sharp; the verse lies above the pan in every key (`#/p/soltan-e-ghalbha`). Checked against transcriptions and left out because their melodies leave the pan: Scarborough Fair (D5 and B-flat4), Zombie (the verse sits a fifth to a seventh below the tonic, under the lowest tone field in every key) and Mad World (its hook is the dorian sixth, B natural in D minor).

The Traditional filter holds fourteen public-domain melodies ported from the Tonalics letter notes (https://tonalics.com/songs), the only openly published handpan-playable notation found: Frère Jacques, Going Home, Hot Cross Buns, Jingle Bells, Jolly Old Saint Nicholas, Lightly Row, London Bridge, Mary Had a Little Lamb, Ode to Joy, Old MacDonald, Sakura Sakura, Twinkle Twinkle Little Star, Up on the Housetop and When the Saints Go Marching In. Each is the melody alone in 4/4, as written or moved by a fifth so that every note sits on the pan, with the tempo taken from the source's stated length. Tonalics' 36 melodies were all tested by code against the pan's ten notes in every key; the other 22 need at least one note it lacks, and Row, Row, Row Your Boat is in 6/8 with half beats the eighths meter cannot hold, so they were left out. Every other handpan notation found online (Master the Handpan, HandpanPal, Handpan ChordBook, Instruments du Monde, ChordU) is paid or all rights reserved and was not copied.

Four public-domain carols follow, with their pitches taken from the engraved scores at piano.org and their rhythm checked against Paul Hardy's Christmas tunebook on abcnotation.com. Both were found through You.com search. A script tested each piano.org melody against the pan's ten notes in every key: **Good King Wenceslas** in F major (`#/p/good-king-wenceslas`), **O Come, O Come, Emmanuel** in D minor (`#/p/o-come-o-come-emmanuel`), **We Three Kings** in 6/8 with the verse in D minor and the refrain in F major (`#/p/we-three-kings`), and **In the Bleak Midwinter** in C major (`#/p/in-the-bleak-midwinter`). C on 9 stands in for every B-flat above A, and one low B in Bleak Midwinter moves up to D. The pieces carry no lyrics. Tested and left out because they need several missing notes: Coventry Carol, God Rest Ye Merry Gentlemen, O Little Town of Bethlehem, Silent Night, Brahms' Lullaby, Shenandoah, Auld Lang Syne, The First Noel and Bach's Minuet in G.

Play the handpan from the Library, Play, or Learn with number keys 0–9 (including the numeric keypad); 0 is the ding and T plays tak. Multiple keys can be struck together. Held keys do not repeat, and shortcuts leave search, form inputs, and Explore controls alone.

A selected piece has one title, a Library link, and Play / Learn mode links. Play has the full score and transport; Learn practices one phrase at a time. Both keep the same selected piece. The handpan and score highlight current and upcoming notes. The header holds Microphone, Mirror, the landscape picker, and Explore. Phones group them under Tools. Escape closes Tools, then returns to the library with its search and filter intact and focus on the piece row.

Microphone is available in Play. The page shows the note you struck and waits until you strike the lit note. Learn uses screen notes, so its Microphone control is disabled with an explanation. Opening a piece or restoring Learn never starts audio or requests microphone access.

Start learning chooses a simple Hands drill from the catalog. Continue learning names the last practiced piece and restores its phrase and tempo. Learn divides the existing score into one-bar phrases. Hear phrase plays the selected bar once, including rests; Try notes waits for your taps on the pan or number keys (T for tak); Repeat slowly loops the same bar at 70% of the original tempo, within the player's 48–140 BPM limits. The practice tempo slider applies to both listening modes. Chords accept each unique note in any order, without a timing score. Screen taps teach note order, not physical handpan accuracy. Rest slots retain their timing in playback and are skipped in Try notes. A fully silent bar explains the rest without starting playback.

Pause and Resume use the existing player's note-based resume: the current note plays again. Restart phrase returns to the bar's start. Next phrase is always a deliberate choice, and completion does not mark anything learned. Mirror preserves the active phrase. Learn's score is a reading surface; score clicks, drags and arrow keys cannot exit Learn or create whole-piece loops. Switching modes or leaving cancels pending practice playback. The legacy step guide is retired; existing `disc-kurd-step` and `disc-kurd-learned` data is preserved, and Play retains optional learned-part checkboxes.

Piece URLs use `#/p/id/play` and `#/p/id/learn`; existing `#/p/id` links still open Play. Direct Learn links restore the saved phrase and tempo for that piece silently. Unknown routes return to the library. Check the pure practice logic and mocked playback with `node scripts/check-practice.mjs`; browser layout, interaction and real-instrument accuracy are separate checks.

On a phone, Enable motion lets you gently tilt and rotate the 3D handpan with the device. Allow motion access when asked, hold the phone comfortably, and use Recenter to reset its neutral position. Tap Motion on to switch it off. Sensor access requires HTTPS or localhost and a browser with device orientation support.

Check motion handling with `node scripts/check-motion.mjs`.

Check the pieces before pushing: `node scripts/check.mjs [candidate-pieces.js]`. It prints every piece and ends with the fault count.

The landscapes and 36 cover illustrations are original artistic interpretations inspired by the [Institute of Ismaili Studies Alamut Valley photographic collection](https://www.iis.ac.uk/multimedia/sites-alamut-valley/), including the narrow fortress ridge, winter mountains, cultivated terraces, and poplar-lined valley. They are not historical reconstructions. Reference photographs are not bundled with the app.

The loading screen offers a simple view with the original library and sheet if 3D takes too long. Static scenery is batched by material. The courtyard breathes at a throttled ambient rate (motes, fireflies, lantern flicker, a slow camera drift, the handpan hovering above its pedestal, and the occasional shooting star); with `prefers-reduced-motion` the scene holds still and redraws only on state changes. Append `?perf=1` to inspect sampled frame timing and renderer counts in the handpan element's `data-profile` attribute.
