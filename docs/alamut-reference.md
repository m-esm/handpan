# Alamut as it was, 1090 to 1256

Facts the 3D landscape in `js/landscape.js` is built from. Each line names its source. Found through You.com search on 2026-10-30.

## The rock

- Conglomerate crag 185 to 220 m above the villages, summit at 2163 m. Juvayni: a kneeling camel with its neck stretched out. Freya Stark: a ship with its nose pointing north-west. (Willey, *Eagle's Nest*, via gateofalamut.com; UNESCO tentative list 5206)
- Ridge runs north-west to south-east, about 140 m long, 9 to 40 m wide. A narrow unbuilt tongue 45 m long and 9 m wide at the north-west end with a swallow's-nest lookout on its north side. At the south-east end a rounded sandstone bluff, the "Onion Castle" (Piyaz Qal'eh), joined to the main castle by a saddle of conglomerate, used for stores and some garrison dwellings, with a wide high tunnel through the rock. (Willey)
- Colour: reddish-brown and grey, striated, with bedding planes dipping steeply. In changing light purple, mauve, metallic grey, brown. The surrounding hills are pastel green, lavender, pinkish, terra cotta, ochre and brown in wide streaks. (Willey; Harji photo essay, simerg.com; Commons photos)
- South flank runs steadily down to the valley, a drop of about 250 m at 45 to 60 degrees. Halfway up it, along its whole length, a horizontal rock-cut water channel 183 m long, 3.6 m wide and 3.3 to 3.9 m deep (2.4 million litres). Four cisterns hewn above it. Two rock-cut stairs from the channel to the top. Nothing is fortified below the channel. (Willey)
- East side falls steeply to a ravine with the goat track. The only approach is the col at the north-west corner where the mule track runs. Two external cisterns cut 15 m down the south-west face. (Willey)
- North: the Hawdeqan range (Siyalan Kuh 3860 m) forms a half circle behind the rock, snow on its heights into summer. Between the range and the rock a smaller hill about 150 m high joined by a narrow neck, probably the quarry. (Willey; Iranica)

## The castle

- Two castles, upper (west, Pileh Qal'eh, 16 m higher) and lower (east), with a walled open field between them. Four towers, east, north, south and west. The great east wall stood 14 m high and 3 m thick with a well-preserved turret faced with hard stone at each end; the only gate passes through it at the north-east corner just below the east tower, through a 6 m rock-cut tunnel; the gatehouse was plastered white inside. Walls elsewhere about 2 m thick and 8 m high, following the rock. (Willey; tappersia.com; cafeerent.com)
- Materials: rubble stone in gypsum mortar, square baked bricks 21 cm by 5 cm, wooden tie beams through the walls, plaster coating, turquoise-glazed and lustre tiles and carved brick on the Mowla Sara (a domed Seljuk-style command building with a mosque). Pointed barrel vaults, flat roofs. Rooms lined with lime plaster for stores. Clay pipes 10 cm wide brought water from a spring to the north. A rock-cut guard room on the south face. (Choubak 2009 excavation report; Willey; UVic thesis on the sister castles)
- A library, an observatory with three south-east windows, and a mint. A vine on the western slope attributed to Hasan-i Sabbah. No paradise garden: Juvayni, who surveyed the castle in 1256 and hated its owners, describes stores and the library and never a garden. (Wikipedia; Willey)

## The valley

- Alamut valley at 1500 to 2100 m, clayey Miocene conglomerates, floods frequent. Mild Caspian-touched climate, 300 to 500 mm of rain, snow lingers only on the heights. (Encyclopaedia Iranica, "Alamut")
- Vegetation: steppe slopes of *Artemisia* and *Astragalus*, hawthorn shrubs between 1000 and 2000 m, no forest zone. Slender poplars line the water and are the signature tree of the valley. Willow, plane and walnut by streams; a plane tree about a thousand years old stands in Gazor Khan square. Cherry orchards are modern. (Iranica; Willey; destinationiran.com; everybodywiki Gazor Khan)
- Farming under Hasan-i Sabbah: terraced slopes and extended irrigation for wheat and barley, rice in small plots on the valley floor, vineyards. Villages on south-facing slopes with flat-roofed houses laid out in steps. (Wikipedia; Iranica)
- Gazor Khan sits at the western foot of the rock, Shotor Khan to the south-east; the Alamut river runs in the main valley to the south, with the Taleqan range and the Chala pass closing the horizon. Ovan lake is a separate small alpine lake some kilometres away. (Willey; Iranica)

## What this means for the scene

Ridge, not cone. Reddish-brown striated rock with a horizontal channel line. Rubble-and-plaster walls, not ashlar. Flat roofs, round towers, one dome with turquoise accents. Poplars and hawthorn, no conifers. Steppe colours, with green only where water runs. A braided river in the valley, not a lake. Snow mountains in a half circle to the north.
